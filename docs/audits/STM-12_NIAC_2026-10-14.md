# STM-12: Stem through niac, 2026-10-14

**Stem build:** v0.27.0. The native binary is from the released
`stem_0.27.0_amd64.deb`, extracted with `dpkg-deb -x` and not installed. It
ran as a loopback-bound daemon with a trial licence.
**niac build:** v0.113.18, the released `niac-0.113.18-linux-amd64.tar.gz`,
run as `niac daemon --once niac0 refl.yaml`.
**Host:** dev-srv-ubuntu (Ubuntu 26.04, x86_64), idle (load average 0.33).

**Outcome.** niac cannot carry a Stem measurement today. Its only way to
impose an authored impairment on a tester's traffic is the `reflector:`
device, and that device answers only the ITO signatures (`DATA:OT`,
`PROBEOT` at payload offset 5). Stem's test master stamps its own signatures
at offset 0, so every Stem test against a niac reflector gets no frame back.
That is filed as niac-go#2654. Measured with ITO probes instead, the niac
reflector adds about 1.1 ms more than its authored delay (niac-go#2655).
The run also showed that every Stem Y.1731 test reports `passed` with no
frame back (#1731), and that a Y.1564 step marks FD and FDV as passed with
no sample (#1732).

## Why a reflector and not a path

STM-12 asks for niac to front a path between Stem and its reflector. niac
does not forward a frame between two real hosts. Its fabric resolves a
destination only to a simulated device (`internal/protocols/ip.go`
`getFabricTarget`), and an address it does not own is dropped as
`no_route`. Placing the Stem reflector behind niac therefore gives no path.
The `reflector:` device is niac's one surface that applies an authored
latency and jitter to a tester's own frames, so it is the only place a
Stem-versus-authored comparison can happen. A transit impairment between
two real hosts would be WAN emulation, which `tc netem` already does and
which is outside niac's device-simulation charter. It is recorded here, not
filed.

## niac's own fidelity, measured with ITO probes

A positive control shows the reflector works when it recognises the frame.
500 UDP probes carrying `PROBEOT` at offset 5 were sent to each device, and
both directions were captured on `niac0`. The delay is reply time minus
request time on niac's own interface, so the veth and the sender add
nothing.

| Device | Authored | Pairs | min | p50 | mean | p99 | max | Outside the band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `reflector-zero` | 0 ms | 500/500 | 0.10 | 0.21 | 0.25 | 0.87 | 2.63 | 4 above 1 ms |
| `reflector-fixed` | 10 ms | 500/500 | 10.23 | 11.07 | 11.11 | 12.93 | 13.94 | 341 outside [10, 11] |
| `reflector` | 10 ± 2 ms | 500/500 | 8.39 | 11.10 | 11.11 | 13.75 | 15.56 | 176 outside [8, 12] |

All values are milliseconds. With the 0.25 ms base path removed, a delayed
reply still leaves about 0.85 ms after its authored time, so the authored
delay is not what a tester measures (niac-go#2655).
